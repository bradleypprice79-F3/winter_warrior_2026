Men of F3 Plainfield, I’m pretty shook up about some news I received this morning. Back in September, I was going to be helping an old friend with a large fish tank removal and install in the Schererville, IN area. My buddy owns and runs an aquarium business for commercial and residential setup and maintenance. On this particular job, the tanks were going to be large and heavy enough that we needed a 4th person.
I reached out to my friend Monica, who I know through my events at Chitown Harley last year, because she lives out that way. She sent me her soon-to-be son-in-law, Tino (engaged to her daughter Ava). Tino came out and worked his butt off…got dirty, stayed late, and never complained…keeping a positive attitude the whole time. I told him, if he approaches this job as sort of an “on the job interview”, it could turn into more steady work and a possible position with the company in the future. He did great. And during the job, I naturally EH’d him to join F3 Northwest Indiana. He was pretty excited about it, and showed up a couple days later. He enjoyed it, even found out he knew a few of the pax…and they gave him the name Nemo of course.
Tino ended up working regularly with my friend over the following weeks on other aquarium jobs, while posting a few times with F3 over the same time period. All seemed on the up and up. He was engaged to the woman he loved, he was going to school full time, he was working side jobs to broaden his experience, he was posting with F3, and was full of youthful energy.
Then I got that news I referred to at the beginning, when Monica called me this morning. On Monday night, at age 25, Santino Martinez (Nemo), took his own life…leaving behind his 7-year old son. Leaving behind his fiancé, who found him. Monica said they had all just had the best weekend ever, where everyone was together and happy. That if somebody had recorded it, they would be amazed at how happy everyone was and that this family had everything.
I didn’t really know Tino. Only met him the one time on that job. But the proximity I had with his family, and the tie-in with F3 makes it hit home a little closer than I can explain. Mental illness is everywhere around us, hidden in plain sight. While friends and family operate around it, business as usual. It’s not just veterans, it’s today’s youth, it’s broken men with seemingly no outlet to pull themselves out of the darkness.
I am telling you all of this, in hopes of raising awareness.
